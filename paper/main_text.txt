                                            Abstract

          The Predictive AI Evaluation Competition (PAIEC) asks for the probability that an
          AI system (the subject) answers a benchmark item correctly, given visible attributes,
          the item text, and labels acquired at budgets of 0­31 per subject­benchmark pair;
          the score is a Brier area under the learning curve (ALC). The official streaming
          client passes one global list with the labels acquired for all sampled pairs to every
          prediction, whereas our earlier submission used only the target pair's labels. We
          now fit a random-effects logistic model to that list once per budget checkpoint: a
          hand-set attribute prior as offset, benchmark, subject and subject­benchmark ef-
          fects, and an exact-item effect learned from other subjects' labels on the same item,
          with probit integration of the posterior uncertainty. Its prior standard deviations
          were chosen by nested, benchmark-held-out simulation of the official protocol on
          the official training release (16,718 pairs, 144 benchmarks), with design and ship
          rule pre-registered. In formative-sized test sets (at most 1,000 subject­item pairs)
          the ALC falls from 0.2095 (our earlier predictor, as the evaluator would run it) to
          0.1966, a paired difference of -0.0129 [-0.0151, -0.0109] (cluster bootstrap over
          135 benchmarks); it is -0.0125 when cross-pair evidence only comes from different
          models and -0.0289 when all pairs of a benchmark are scored together. Bench-
          mark difficulty and exact-item difficulty contribute about equally. On a 17-pair
          testbed of four small open models the difference is -0.0074 [-0.0203, +0.0032]
          for the pre-registered acquisition trajectory (not significant) and significant in 4
          of 5 trajectories. An acquisition rule that skips items already labelled for another
          subject changes the ALC by a further -0.0005 [-0.0009, -0.0002]. The predictor is
          standard-library Python; one fit of a 62,000-label list takes 1.68 s and a prediction
          0.116 ms. Earlier negative results (a fitted attribute prior, a pre-registered item-text
          prior) are summarised. Code and text were produced with an LLM coding assistant
          (Claude).

1 Introduction

Evaluating every new AI system on every benchmark is expensive, and the number of benchmarks
keeps growing [Liang et al., 2023]. Recent work therefore predicts performance instead of measuring
it: from a few hundred informative items selected with item response theory (IRT) [Polo et al.,
2024, Kipnis et al., 2025, Perlitz et al., 2024], adaptively per model [Hofmann et al., 2025, Zhuang
et al., 2025], from other models' scores [Ruan et al., 2024, Ye et al., 2023], or from a few labelled
instances of the new model [Pacchiardi et al., 2024]. PAIEC [Stanford AIMS Lab, 2026] turns this
into a forecasting task with a precise interface: predict([subject, item], labeled) returns
Pr(correct), and labeled holds the labels acquired so far, at budgets of n  {0, 1, 3, 7, 15, 31}
labels per subject­benchmark pair.

Our submission for rounds 1­3 read labeled as the target pair's own labels: a hand-set attribute
prior, beta-binomial shrinkage of the pair rate [Efron and Morris, 1975], and item-level corrections
gated by a leave-one-out test. It beat the organisers' empirical mean on two local testbeds and on the
training release, while two attempts to improve the budget-0 prior with the release--a fitted attribute
prior and a pre-registered item-text prior--failed under benchmark-held-out cross-validation. The
diagnosis was that the residual error is dominated by benchmark difficulty, which nothing visible at
budget 0 reveals. Reading the official streaming client closely (Section 2) shows that the information
is there from budget 1 on: labeled is one global list with the labels of all sampled pairs. Other
subjects' labels on the same benchmark reveal its difficulty, a subject's labels on other benchmarks its
ability, and because acquisition and evaluation pools are split per pair, the very item being predicted is
often labelled for another subject. The rules allow exactly this ("the submitted program may use the
attributes and acquired labels supplied through the evaluation interface to adapt during evaluation").

Contributions. (1) A precise statement of the evaluator's global-evidence protocol and a faithful
multi-pair simulator of it on the official training release, with benchmark-held-out folds and five
test-set configurations. (2) A random-effects (Rasch-style) predictor fitted to the global list once
per checkpoint [Lord, 1980, Breslow and Clayton, 1993], with an exact-item effect and probit
integration [MacKay, 1992]; pre-registered selection and ship rule; paired, cluster-bootstrapped gains
at every budget; component ablations; a check that the shipped files reproduce the simulator inside
the organisers' own client. (3) A coverage-oriented acquisition rule. (4) The earlier per-pair predictor,
kept as fallback and for budget 0, and its negative results (Appendices B­D).

Related work and design choices. Item response models--a subject ability and an item difficulty
on a logit scale--are widely used for LLM benchmark data, to rank systems, find informative items
and estimate full-benchmark scores from few items [Rodriguez et al., 2021, Polo et al., 2024, Kipnis
et al., 2025]. These methods fit item parameters offline on hundreds of models; in PAIEC the test
benchmarks are new, so difficulty must be learned during evaluation from a few labels of a few
subjects. We therefore keep the Rasch structure but replace free parameters by random effects with
Gaussian priors (a generalised linear mixed model [Breslow and Clayton, 1993]), which shrinks a
benchmark's difficulty towards the prior when it has one or three labels and lets it speak when it
has hundreds, and we use one difficulty per item only where the item itself was labelled. Adaptive
testing [Hofmann et al., 2025, Zhuang et al., 2025] chooses the next item from the current ability
estimate; the PAIEC stream only lets a policy accept or skip a random candidate, and uncertainty-
based skipping did not help in round 1 (Appendix E), so our acquisition rule only maximises coverage
of distinct items. Work that predicts a model's scores from other models' scores [Ruan et al., 2024,
Ye et al., 2023] motivates pooling across subjects; our model does this at the item level and inside
one evaluation run.

2 Task, interface and the global label list

A subject is a dictionary of strings (normalized_name, provider, release_date, access_date,
harness, harness_version, reasoning_effort, subject_features_extra); an item has
item_content, item_features, interactors and an anonymous benchmark_id. Each subject­

benchmark pair with at least 80 distinct items is split 50/50 into an acquisition and an evaluation pool,

fixed across submissions. Acquisition candidates of a pair arrive in a fixed random order; an optional
acquisition_function returns True to reveal a label (31 per pair at most), otherwise labels are

drawn uniformly. After 0, 1, 3, 7, 15 and 31 labels per pair every evaluation item is predicted, giving

Brier scores [Brier, 1950] B0, . . . , B31 and

ALC = 0.1 B0 + 0.2 B1 + 0.2 B3 + 0.2 B7 + 0.2 B15 + 0.1 B31,  (1)

averaged over pairs (lower is better). A formative evaluation samples at most 1,000 subject­item
pairs (acquisition and evaluation pools together) from benchmarks of the private test pool; runs have
8 hours.

The global label list. In the organisers' client (tools/streaming_ingestion.py,
run_streaming) every coordinator event carries one list, labeled = event["labeled"];
at an evaluate event all distinct inputs of the checkpoint are predicted with
prediction_stream(model_path, unique, labeled, ...), i.e. with the same list, and the

2
competition page states that labeled "contains [[subject, item], label] entries across sampled
subject­benchmark pairs". At budget n the list therefore holds up to n labels of every pair; at budget
0 it is empty. Our rounds 1­3 predictor, like the organisers' empirical-mean baseline, used only the
target pair's labels; it selected them by benchmark and model name, so as deployed it also pooled the
labels of other subjects with the same name (other reasoning efforts, harnesses or test conditions).
Using the other entries is permitted; what is forbidden is extracting test data or trying to identify the
anonymous subjects and benchmarks, and we do neither: the model only uses anonymous keys (the
subject's full attribute dictionary, benchmark_id, item text) inside one evaluation run and keeps
nothing across runs.

3 Method

Budget-0 prior (unchanged). o(s, i) = clip(zsubj(s) + zitem(i), ±1.5), a hand-set logistic form:
release year, reasoning effort, parameter count or size words in the name, and harness for the subject;
tier/level/points cues in item_features and item length for the item (Appendix B). At budget 0 the
prediction is (o).

Cross-pair random-effects model. For a label ye of subject s on item i of benchmark b in the
global list, stage 1 is the logistic random-effects model

          logit Pr(ye = 1) = o(s, i) + µ + vb + us + wsb,  (2)

with independent priors µ  N (0, µ2), vb  N (0, b2), us  N (0, s2), wsb  N (0, s2b), subjects
identified by their complete attribute dictionary (as the organisers' baseline does) and benchmarks
by benchmark_id. We compute the MAP estimate by backfitting: one Newton step per effect type
and sweep, g  g + eg(ye - pe) - g/ 2 / eg pe(1 - pe) + 1/ 2 , at most 25 sweeps
(penalised quasi-likelihood for a GLMM [Breslow and Clayton, 1993]). The target's latent logit

m = o + µ^ + v^b + u^s + w^sb (zero for groups without labels) has approximate posterior variance
V = g 1/(Hg + g-2), with g2 for an unseen group, and p =  m/ 1 + V /8 [MacKay, 1992].
Exact-item effect (stage 2). If the target item (same benchmark_id and text) was labelled for other
subjects, an item effect d  N (0, d2) is estimated from those labels by 4 Newton steps on the
conditional scale (their stage-1 logits multiplied by c = 1 + d2/8, since stage 1 averages over
item difficulty), and p =  (c m + d^)/ 1 +  Var(d)/8 with m the probit-scaled stage-1 logit;

without matches this reduces to the stage-1 prediction. The model also has slots for a model-name

effect and a name­benchmark effect, which the selection switched off. Shipped prior standard

deviations: µ = 0.5, b = 1.5, s = 0.5, sb = 0.6, d = 2.25 (Section 4).

Engineering. labeled is identical for all calls of a checkpoint, so the parsed list and the fit are
cached at module level under a fingerprint (length and a hash of about 70 evenly spaced entries) and
replaced atomically under a lock; separate worker processes fit once each. A list of 62,000 labels
is fitted in 1.68 s; a cache-hit call takes 0.116 ms (Table 4). The code is standard-library Python,
deterministic, and falls back to the rounds 1­3 predictor on any failure.

Fallback: the per-pair predictor of rounds 1­3. Beta-binomial shrinkage of the pair rate towards
the prior (0 = 4 pseudo-counts) and, from 5 labels, a TF-IDF nearest-neighbour correction and
(from 7 labels) a ridge adjustment on item features, each switched on only when a leave-one-out test
on the pair's labels shows an improvement of 2 standard errors (Appendix B). It handles budget 0 and
any failure of the cross-pair model.

Acquisition: fresh first. With cross-pair learning, a label on an item nobody has labelled can help

every other subject whose evaluation pool contains that item, whereas a second label on an already
labelled item adds less. labeling.py therefore accepts candidates in stream order (a uniformly
random sample, as the default policy) except that it skips a candidate whose item is already in the
global list for another subject while labels_remaining < items_remaining, so the budget is always
spent. The function has no prediction parameter, so the client does not call predict for every
candidate.

4 Evaluation protocol

Frame. The official training release (aims-foundations/measurement-db, revision
54074453da) gives 16,718 pairs of (subject, test condition) on 144 benchmarks with at least 20
items (16,303 with 80), 3,118,742 item-level labels (mean over trials) and 165,764 distinct items.
Each pair keeps its 200 answered items with the lowest benchmark-level hash rank, so subjects of a
benchmark share items as far as their answered sets allow. The release is dominated by test-condition
variants of one model: 92.7% of pairs share (model name, benchmark) with another pair.

Simulator. The five benchmark folds of round 2 hold out whole benchmarks. A test set is a set
of pairs of one fold that shares one global list. Per pair (fixed across test sets) items are split 50/50
and the acquisition pool is permuted at random; budget n is its first n labels (the official random
policy yields a uniformly random subset in stream order); at budget n every prediction of the test
set receives the union of all its pairs' first n labels. Configurations: A (primary, formative-like):
1­3 random benchmarks, 10 random pairs with at least 80 items split over them, 100 items per pair
(1,000 subject­item pairs), 300 test sets per fold; E: as A but no two pairs share a model name,
so cross-pair evidence comes only from different models; B: all pairs of one benchmark (up to 300,
200 items each); C: up to 2,000 pairs of a whole fold in one list; D: A plus up to two pairs with
20­79 items that are evaluated on all items and never acquired (the page does not say how such pairs
are handled). Per-pair ALC; means over (test set, pair) units; 95% CIs by a cluster bootstrap over
benchmarks (2,000 resamples).

Pre-registration and selection. experiments/results/round4/PREREG.md (SHA-256 prefix
b9e95a5b47e9, consistent with every result file) fixed configurations A­D, predictors, the grid of
864 settings (b, name, s, name,b, sb, d, probit on/off; µ = 0.5), nested selection (for each outer
fold, the best setting on the other four folds' config-A test sets), the primary outcome and the ship rule
before any outcome was computed: ship iff (a) the config-A ALC difference to our earlier predictor
as deployed (S0) has a CI below zero, (b) the real testbed (Section 5) is not significantly worse, (c) no
other configuration is significantly worse, and (d) engineering gates (never raises, smoke test and
validator pass, a 62,000-label checkpoint in 60 s). The selected setting lay on the grid boundary in
b and d; an addendum written after the primary result (consistent) added configuration E and an
extended grid of 2,160 settings, selected on A and E jointly; it returned the same setting (Appendix A).
The stdlib model.py and the vectorised simulator agree to 1.4 × 10-5 on 56,750 predictions.

Local testbeds. The real testbed has 17 pairs: four small open models (Qwen2.5-Instruct
1.5B/3B/7B, Llama-3.2-3B) run locally with Ollama on GSM8K, SVAMP and a BIG-Bench-Hard
mix under two harnesses (Appendix C); for round 4 all pairs share one global list. A synthetic IRT
testbed was used in rounds 1­3 only.

Real testbed. With one global list over the 17 real pairs (seed 0, as pre-registered), XP reaches
0.2222 against 0.2295 for our earlier predictor as deployed: -0.0074 [-0.0203, +0.0032] (not signifi-
cant; rule (b) holds); over five acquisition trajectories the difference ranges from -0.0138 to -0.0074
and is significant in 4. The deployed name filter pools the two harnesses of the same model; a per-pair
filter would change its ALC by -0.0071 [-0.0164, +0.0009] (not significant); against that per-pair
predictor XP changes the ALC by -0.0002 [-0.0072, +0.0074] (seeds: -0.0047 to -0.0002). Three
benchmarks with four to seven pairs each give little cross-pair evidence, and at n = 1 XP is worse
here (0.2549 vs. 0.2468).

Acquisition. In configuration A the fresh-first rule changes the XP ALC by -0.0005 [-0.0009,
-0.0002] (significant), essentially all of it at n = 15 (-0.0017) and n = 31 (-0.0017), where item
matches matter; it does not affect S0 (+0.0001 [-0.0001, +0.0003]). On the real testbed (seed 0) it
changes the ALC by +0.0001 [-0.0010, +0.0011] (not significant; over five seeds: mean -0.0019,
significantly better in 2, significantly worse in 1). Under the pre-registered rule it is shipped.

Engineering. Table 4: even 4,000 pairs per checkpoint take well under a minute on one core
(rule (d)). Inside the organisers' own streaming client, driven by a local mock coordinator on the
real testbed, the shipped files run in stream acquisition mode with 0 predict calls during 1,135
acquisition decisions, and the resulting ALC equals the simulator's to machine precision. The smoke
test adds 220 cross-pair cases (unseen subjects and benchmarks, malformed entries, determinism, four
threads, a forced fit failure, a 62,000-label list). The ZIP (19.8 kB, SHA-256 prefix c76331d084f1)
passes the official validator. Decision: the cross-pair model and the fresh-first rule are shipped.

Earlier rounds in brief. On the local testbeds the per-pair predictor beat the organisers' empirical
mean (real: 0.2224 vs. 0.2871; synthetic: 0.1797 vs. 0.2208), mostly through shrinkage; its item-level
components and the acquisition policies changed the ALC by less than 0.005; on the release a fitted
attribute prior and a pre-registered item-text prior (-0.0000 [-0.0013, +0.0014]) did not beat the
hand-set prior (Appendices C­H). If the evaluator passed only the target pair's labels, XP would still
be slightly better on the release (-0.0016 [-0.0028, -0.0005] against S1) but worse on the real testbed
(+0.0060 [+0.0022, +0.0099]).

6 Limitations

Test-set composition is unknown. The gain depends on how many pairs share a list and how they
overlap; configurations A­E bracket plausible cases, and the gain is positive in all of them, but
formative sets may be smaller or contain one pair per benchmark, where only the subject effect and
the fallback-like pair shrinkage remain. Release structure. Most release pairs are test-condition
variants of one model; configuration E removes that structure and the gain persists, but the selected
d was tuned on the mixture. Item identity is exact text match within a benchmark; paraphrased or
condition-specific item texts give no match. Pairs with fewer than 80 items are handled under one
assumed rule (D). Real testbed. 17 pairs of small models on three benchmarks give wide CIs; there
the gain over a correct per-pair filter is not significant, and XP is worse at n = 1. Budget 0 still rests
on the hand-set prior, whose weaknesses for frontier subjects are analysed in Appendix I. Acquisition
gains are small and mixed on the real testbed.

B Per-pair predictor (rounds 1­3; now the fallback and the budget-0 prior)

All constants below are hand-set and are still shipped in model.py: the budget-0 prior is used by the
cross-pair model, the rest is its fallback. The few whose values were adjusted after looking at the
local testbeds are marked  and have sensitivity analyses in Appendix D.

Budget-0 prior. A logistic prior p0 = (z) with z = clip(zsubj +zitem, ±1.5), so p0  [0.18, 0.82].
The subject part is zsubj = 0.7 · (0.35 clip(t - 2024.5, ±2) + e + 0.25 clip(log2(N/30B), ±4) +
0.1 [non-default harness]), where t is the release year (fractional), e  {0, 0.15, 0.3, 0.45} encodes
an empty, low, other or high reasoning_effort, and N is a parameter count parsed from the name
("7b", "70B"); when no count is present, name tokens such as mini/nano/flash contribute -0.3 and
pro/opus/ultra contribute 0.2. An intercept (0) and a per-provider table (0 entries) complete the subject
part; both are empty in the shipped prior and are the slots a fitted coefficient table would fill. The item
part parses key=value pairs in item_features: a tier/difficulty/level value matching hard/expert/. . .
adds -0.6, easy/basic/. . . adds 0.5, a numeric level   [1, 5] adds -0.25( - 3), and points above
one subtract 0.1 each (capped at five); finally the item length adds -0.12 clip(log2(L/600), ±3) for
L characters. The directions encode common knowledge (newer, larger, reasoning-enabled subjects
do better; harder, longer, higher-value items are failed more often); the magnitudes were chosen to
move the prior by at most a few tenths of a logit per attribute and were not tuned on either testbed.

Pair-level shrinkage. Given n acquired labels y1, . . . , yn of the same pair with item logits zi(tje)m
and mean z¯, the prior rate is r = (clip(zsubj + z¯)) and the posterior mean of a Beta­binomial model
with prior strength 0 = 4 pseudo-counts is  = (0r + j yj)/(0 + n) [Efron and Morris, 1975].
The pair estimate for a target item is ppair = (logit  + zitem - z¯), i.e. the item cues shift the pair
rate on the logit scale.

Item-level component 1: ridge features. From n  7 labels, three cheap features of the item
text--log length, digit density and log(1+number of sentences)--are standardised over the acquired
items and a logistic regression with offset logit ppair and ridge penalty  = 4 is fitted by six Newton
steps; its adjustment clip(f~, ±1) is added to the target's logit.

Item-level component 2: TF-IDF nearest neighbours. Item texts (content, features and interactors,

where the anchor a is the pair estimate (optionally including the ridge adjustment) and  = 2 by

items share many terms; Appendix E compares a sharper unigram-only representation.

Leave-one-out gating. From n  5 labels, every configuration in {ridge off, on} × { 
{0, 1, 2, 4}} ( = 0 means no kNN) is scored by its leave-one-out Brier score on the acquired
labels themselves: label j is predicted from the other n - 1 labels, including a leave-one-out shrink-
age estimate and a leave-one-out ridge fit. The best configuration replaces plain shrinkage only if its
mean per-item improvement exceeds 2 standard error(s) of that mean; otherwise the predictor returns
ppair. This makes adaptation opt-in per pair and budget: a pair whose labels show no exploitable
structure is predicted by shrinkage alone. Below 5 labels no item-level component is used at all (in
preliminary runs an always-on kNN from one or three labels only added noise at B1 and B3).

Acquisition policy. labeling.py implements the streaming interface and never raises. Candidates
are accepted in stream order (uniformly random, hence the same label distribution as the evaluator's
default) except that a candidate whose maximum TF-IDF cosine similarity to an already acquired
item exceeds 0.6 is skipped while fewer than 90% of the remaining candidates are needed; it
always accepts when the remaining candidates are needed to spend the budget. We compare this
novelty filter with the evaluator's random policy, plain stream order, the organisers' uncertainty band
|p - 0.5|  0.15 [Truong et al., 2026], and a novelty-plus-uncertainty variant (skip |p - 0.5| > 0.45
once 7 labels are known).

better prior, i.e. training data, can reduce it. On the real testbed the gap is 0.0266 (0.2228 vs. 0.1961).
The two parts of the headroom are of similar size but call for different remedies: a prior fitted to real
subjects and benchmarks, and item difficulty information from outside the pair.

E Additional ablations

Prior strength. Table 14 gives the ALC for 0  {1, 2, 4, 8}. The best value differs between
testbeds (shrinkage: 2 on synthetic, 8 on real), reflecting the wider spread of pair rates in the synthetic
generator; the ALC of the full model varies by 0.0038 (synthetic) and 0.0182 (real) over the range, so
the choice matters at the third decimal. On the training release 0 = 4 gives 0.2051 against 0.2052
(2) and 0.2086 (8).

When does text help? The synthetic sweep (Figure 3, right; Table 13) varies how much difficulty
is explained by topic. With no topic or length signal (topic = 0.00,  = 0.0) the full model's paired
difference to shrinkage is +0.0002 [-0.0000, +0.0005], the gate's cost when there is nothing to find; at
the strongest setting (topic = 1.00) it is -0.0002 [-0.0005, +0.0001], while the item-level headroom
(rate oracle minus true-probability oracle) grows from 0.0080 to 0.0306.

Gating. Replacing the LOO gate by always-on components at their default settings gives 0.1808
versus 0.1797 (synthetic; paired gated-always -0.0011 [-0.0017, -0.0005], significant) and 0.2203
versus 0.2224 (real; +0.0021 [-0.0009, +0.0048], not significant). The gate pays where always-on
components overfit (the synthetic ridge) and costs a little where a component carries real signal (on
the real testbed longer GSM8K problems are failed more often, which the always-on ridge exploits at
n  15); the second effect is within noise. Requiring 0, 1 or 2 standard errors of LOO improvement
gives full-model ALCs of 0.1808, 0.1799 and 0.1797 (synthetic) and 0.2219, 0.2223 and 0.2224
(real); we ship the most conservative setting.

Sentence embeddings. The embeddings were computed offline for the real testbed (the model is
on the validator's allow-list via models.txt, so it could have been shipped). They change the full
model's ALC from 0.2224 to 0.2223 (paired -0.0001 [-0.0005, +0.0001], not significant) and the
always-on kNN variant from 0.2226 to 0.2252 (+0.0026 [+0.0000, +0.0055], significant), at the cost
of a neural dependency, model loading and about 10 ms per embedding on CPU.

Sharper lexical similarity does not help. A unigram-only TF-IDF whose target terms are restricted
to the acquired vocabulary separates same-topic from other-topic synthetic items far better (mean
cosine about 0.3 versus 0.1 in the generator) but makes the always-on kNN worse: paired against
the shipped representation, the always-on kNN changes by +0.0009 [-0.0001, +0.0020] (synthetic)

Acquisition policies. Table 10 compares policies for the full predictor on identical pools and
streams. Plain stream order differs from the evaluator's random policy by -0.0013 [-0.0038, +0.0013]

that similar--yet its ALC equals that of plain stream order to four decimals (0.2180 vs. 0.2180):
skipping a near-duplicate changes which uniformly random items are labelled, not their distribution.
The organisers' uncertainty band gives +0.0001 [-0.0027, +0.0030] and -0.0041 [-0.0096, +0.0016],
and the novelty-plus-uncertainty variant -0.0013 and -0.0044: selecting by |p - 0.5| reorders a
near-random sample at small budgets and, once item-level corrections exist, biases it towards items
of intermediate predicted difficulty, distorting the pair-rate estimate that carries most of the metric.
None of the policies is significantly different from random on either testbed; we ship the one that is
closest to random while removing redundant items.

F Replication

Regenerating the synthetic testbed with two further seeds gives full-model ALCs of 0.2266 and
0.1963 (empirical mean 0.2737 and 0.2438; shrinkage 0.2264 and 0.1963). On the real testbed, four
further acquisition trajectories give full-model ALCs between 0.2127 and 0.2240 (empirical mean

3 × 3 grid over the kNN scale and anchor (  {1, 2, 4},   {1, 2, 4}) spans 0.1806­0.1809 on a
smaller synthetic generator, i.e. the kernel constants matter little.

G Fitting pipeline details

fit_prior_from_measurement_db.py runs in five steps, each a sub-command. plan: list
the repository, download the small benchmarks table of every directory and keep those
with response_type = binary and granularity = item (the organisers' eligibility rule in
tools/prepare_data.py); record the bytes still to download and stop above a cap (30 GB by
default). download: fetch the eligible subjects, items and responses tables into the organisers'
payload layout. fit: build one row per (subject, test condition, item) with the mean response over trials
as label (fractional labels enter as two weighted rows), each pair receiving total weight one; compute
the subject and item terms with the shipped model.subject_terms and model.item_terms, so
that fitted coefficients map one-to-one onto the constants of Appendix B; add a one-hot for providers
with at least 15 pairs and one for each training benchmark; fit an L2-penalised logistic regression.
Five-fold cross-validation holds out whole benchmarks; the penalty C and the logit cap are selected
by the pair-weighted budget-0 Brier score of the prior on held-out pairs, and 0  {2, 4, 8} by the
closed-form shrinkage ALC of the protocol (50/50 split, 31 random acquisitions, Eq. 1) on held-out
pairs with at least 80 items, paired against the hand-set prior on the same pairs. testbeds: evaluate the
fitted table inside the harness on both local testbeds against the shipped constants. dry-run: generate
tables with the release schema (shared pool of subjects, random subsets per benchmark, benchmark
difficulty offsets with standard deviation one logit, labels from a known coefficient table over the
prior's own terms, two ineligible benchmarks) and run fit on them. The dry run used 14 eligible
benchmarks, 299 pairs and 47,784 rows; a light-regularisation fit recovers all 13 slope signs with
maximum error 0.05 and mean error 0.02 logits, and the fitted benchmark effects have standard devia-
tion 0.57. Its cross-validated selection chose C = 0.003 and 0 = 4 and did not beat the hand-set
prior (budget-0 Brier +0.0036 [+0.0004, +0.0069]; shrinkage ALC +0.0013 [+0.0001, +0.0027]), so
on these tables the rule would have kept the hand-set constants.
